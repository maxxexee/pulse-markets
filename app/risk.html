Risk Disclosure

Last updated 17 November 2026

An attention index measures how much a topic was written about. It is not a price, and it is not a forecast. Read the cards on this site with the following in mind.

The measurement is narrow

A daily score comes from two keyless sources: English Wikipedia pageviews and Hacker News story counts. A topic that trends on television, TikTok or in another language can score flat here. A story that spikes only on Hacker News can lift an entity that most people never saw.

Each day is final, and can still be imperfect

Wikipedia publishes a day's pageviews after that day ends, and the counts can be revised. PULSE refuses to publish below 50 percent source coverage, and it never overwrites an already published day, so a thin day stays thin in the record.

Momentum compares two published days

A percentage on a card is the change in attention level against the epoch one week earlier. If that earlier epoch is unavailable, the card shows a dash rather than zero. A large percentage on a small base is still a small number of readers.

Nothing here is financial advice

No part of this site is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold anything. Prediction markets involve the risk of losing the whole amount staked, and this preview does not let you stake anything at all.

Verify before you rely on it

Every card links to the underlying Wikipedia article or Hacker News search, and every snapshot is published with a signature and its coverage figures. Check those before quoting a number.
